Step inside the game.

AC VR Hub is a free, open-source Windows app that brings arcade and console light gun and racing games into VR. It runs on your desk and as a panel inside SteamVR: pick a game, press Play, and it opens in the headset. Bring your own games; none are included.

New game profiles and emulator wrappers are added all the time.

How you play

Your hands are the cabinet.

Your motion controllers stand in for whatever the cabinet had: a light gun, a wheel to grab, or a pointer.

Light guns

Point and shoot. The shot lands where the barrel points, you reload by pointing off the screen, and you feel the recoil. Each game gets the gun it was made for, held in a grip hand.

Racing

Racers steer with the stick and pedal with the triggers by default. Roll and tilt steering, and Model 3's two-handed wheel, are opt-in views, never the default.

Rules from real headset sessions

Game controls stay off the left Menu and the right headset button, since SteamVR and the headset own them; the left Menu opens the SteamVR dashboard, where the Hub panel is. Every game uses the same stick clicks: Left stick click = Start. Right stick click = Select or Coin. Click both sticks together = put the screen in front of you again. Hold both stick clicks for 3 seconds = quit and save. Exit game in the Hub panel also ends the game cleanly (not yet checked in the headset).

Four ways to see a game

  • Real 3D: the game's own world drawn in true depth, one picture per eye.
  • Inside the game: you sit in the game's camera and look around with your head.
  • Open frame: the picture is not boxed to an old 4:3 screen; the world carries on past its edges where the game draws it.
  • Big screen: a large flat screen in a room, with the gun in your hand.

Gun models

The right gun in your hand.

Every gun, controller and control on this page is the project's own model, the same file the Hub's setups draw in the headset. Twelve of the fifteen built guns and controllers are new designs: nine from 21 October, reworked after a four-specialist studio review, and three special guns (an energy gun, a heavy revolver and a cutter) that are built and wired in but not yet given to a game. The grip hands now put the index finger on every pistol's trigger. None of the new shapes or the new hand pose has a headset verdict yet.

Which gun for which game

  • PlayStation: the slim console pistol
  • PlayStation 2: the D-pad console pistol
  • Saturn: the chunky revolver
  • Wii: the remote shell, with a pointer remote for motion games
  • Namco arcade: the blowback pistol; Sega arcade: the twin pistol
  • Mounted-gun cabinets: the heavy machine gun

Parts that move

Each model names its moving parts: the trigger, the slide that kicks back on a shot, the revolver cylinder, buttons and sticks. The hosts drive them from your controller, so the gun in your hand reacts the way the cabinet's did.

Twelve guns and controllers are built as models so far; the project's gun data lists 27, and the rest follow.

Sit-down racers

Ghost controls.

Racing cabinets appear around you as see-through controls where your hands and feet go: the wheel, shifter and pedals on the racer beside these panels are the project's own models.

Wheels and shifters

A three-spoke wheel, a sequential shifter and a four-gate shifter, each with its own grab points.

Pedals, bars and sticks

Accelerator and brake pedals, motorcycle handlebars, an arcade stick and a flight stick, plus button panels.

Sixteen control models

All original and CC0. Six of them are on the model bench above, listed after the guns.

Prize counter

Everything is a file.

Games, VR setups, install recipes, control sets, guns and profiles are plain TOML you can read, copy, share and override. Adding a game takes a new folder and no code.

Built on the people doing this work

Supermodel, MAME, PCSX2, Dolphin and DolphinXR, Flycast, SwanStation, Beetle, mupen64plus, Visual Pinball X, Time Crisis VR, namco22-decompile and many more. We provide VR patches and some installation recipes. Most integrations still need a separately prepared build; setup sources and patches are kept in the repo.

No game content

AC VR Hub never ships, downloads or links to ROMs, BIOS files or disc images. You bring your own legally obtained games; the Hub reads them where they are.

Licences

The Hub is GPL-3.0-only. The shared VR runtime, libacvr, is MIT so any emulator or engine can link it. The project's own gun, hand and control models are CC0; one adapted gun model is CC BY 4.0 and credited in the repo. The ASCII games on the cabinet screens come from ascii-magic-art (MIT). Website notices cover Three.js, the ASCII art, the fonts and the separate music rights; the privacy page says what the site does not collect.

The Hub

One front door.

The Qt Hub for Windows is the only way games are started. It is a desktop window and also a SteamVR dashboard panel, so you can pick the next game without taking the headset off.

The SteamVR panel

A Steam-like grid of system tabs with a game page, a large on-panel Back button and guards against double presses on Play.

Your library, read in place

The Hub finds your games up to six folders deep without moving or copying anything. Artwork is fetched only when you ask, per game or per system.

Nothing left running

When a game ends, a watchdog ends the helper programs it started and never touches anything else on your PC. Games can also appear in your Steam library.

Where it stands.

Most games work in hidden offline proofs (E2): controls reach the game and pictures are checked. A game only counts as accepted (E3) once the developer has played it in the headset.

  • Sega Rally 2 DX inside the game, with the wheel
  • Time Crisis on the big screen (MAME)

Still open

  • Controls for PS2 games that use no gun
  • Universal stick-click controls (Start, Select or Coin, recentre, quit): changed, not yet checked in the headset
  • Easier system switching in the Hub, and one layout for desk and headset
  • A first tester release (0.01): not published yet
