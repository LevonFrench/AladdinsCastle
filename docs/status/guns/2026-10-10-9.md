## Checker guards

Actual-asset checking now rejects:

- Unsupported logical motion drives; `button:` needs a nonempty suffix.
- Present durations that are not integers in 1..10000 ms, including bool/float.
- Multiple drivers targeting the same canonical or LOD1 node. Distinct targets
  may share a logical drive, matching native behavior.
- Motions targeting a static reference or any of its ancestors: exact grip,
  second grip and muzzle names, and every sight/effect prefix. This includes
  indirect ancestry and either LOD target.
- Non-table motion metadata, empty motion IDs or more than 64 entries.
- Material RGBA with a wrong shape, nonnumeric/bool/nonfinite/out-of-range values,
  plus unsupported PBR texture references even if no texture table was declared.
- Decoded vertex RGB/RGBA with a mismatched layout/count or invalid finite/unit
  range. Float colors and normalized unsigned byte/short colors are supported;
  RGB receives alpha 1. Material alpha below 1 remains valid.

Authored-tier requirements remain deliberately stricter than the general native
decoder: four used material roles and two explicit semantic LOD aliases. The
checker supplements the native acceptance gate; it does not replace it.

## Required source naming correction

The new ancestry review exposed an export conflict: visible `sight_front_mesh`
and `sight_rear_mesh` would export with the reserved `sight_` prefix below slide
or whole-body recoil. Native decoding protects those names too and would reject
the slide/twin assets. Per explicit lead direction, this follow-up renames only
the visible parts to `front_sight_geometry`/`rear_sight_geometry`.

Static `sight_front`/`sight_rear` nodes, reference transforms, part coordinates,
materials and motion hierarchy are preserved. Updated source movement tests and
a seven-plan regression check that animated visible meshes do not claim reserved
native reference names or prefixes. Ordinary grip-rib geometry remains valid.
